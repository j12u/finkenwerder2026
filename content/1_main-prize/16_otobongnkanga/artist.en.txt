Title: Otobong Nkanga

----

Text:

(*1974 in Kano, Nigeria)

Artistic practice lies the relationship between humanity and the earth, and the materials extracted from it from plants and seeds to rare earths and minerals. Guided by the idea that energy is never lost but continuously transformed and sustained in other forms, the artist creates compelling images of transformation and metamorphosis in drawings, watercolours, installations, large-scale tapestries, and performances. Observing the Anthropocene through a critical lens, Nkanga reflects on its far-reaching consequences while simultaneously revealing nature’s resilience and capacity for adaptation, reminding us that the earth will endure beyond humankind. Through her engagement with Indigenous knowledge systems, as well as with the architectures and mythologies of the African continent, Nkanga challenges inherited European ways of thinking and contemporary political developments in the Global North. In their place, she proposes alternative modes of coexistence and economic exchange. For her first solo exhibition in Hamburg, the Antwerp-based artist focuses on the two major research-based works Contained Measures of a Kolanut and Tsumeb Fragments. Accompanied by related tapestries, documentary photographs, and videos, the exhibition opens a multilayered space for reflection on landscapes and memory, identity and belonging, and the way in which resources are extracted, traded, and understood. Nkanga develops a poetic visual language that powerfully evokes the intricate relationship between humanity and nature.
