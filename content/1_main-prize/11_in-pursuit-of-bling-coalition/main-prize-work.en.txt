Description: This photographic series brings together images of archives, models, minerals, and collages. Each work captures a thought or a memory while simultaneously serving as a point of departure for the development of further works. Again and again, Nkanga’s investigations revolve around the value of natural resources, questions of geology, and the use of land. As part of her research into the Green Hill mine in Tsumeb, Otobong Nkanga also visited the Museum für Naturkunde in Berlin. The museum houses an extensive mineralogical collection from Tsumeb that was transported to Berlin during the period of German colonial expansion for scientific study. In this context, the collection of mineral samples appears as an early precursor to large-scale colonial extraction and exploitation. Biotite and muscovite belong to the mica group of minerals. Used in cosmetics and industry, they lend surfaces their characteristic shimmer. Nkanga reflects on the historical and contemporary entanglements surrounding the extraction and processing of such lustrous substances, deepening her exploration of the ambiguous meanings of shine, surface, and appearance.
